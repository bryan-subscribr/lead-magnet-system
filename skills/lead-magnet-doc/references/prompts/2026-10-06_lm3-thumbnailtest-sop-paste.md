1. Hourly Testing (Thumbnailtest) vs. Daily Only (TubeBuddy)
[Thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) offers hourly testing, while TubeBuddy only provides daily testing
Hourly testing allows us to find winning thumbnails and titles faster for new uploads, which is critical during the first 24 hours of a video's performance
With hourly testing, you can optimise thumbnails within the first few hours of upload rather than sacrificing a full day's performance with a potentially underperforming thumbnail
2. Multiple Variations Testing

* [Thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) allows testing multiple variations simultaneously (unlimited variations)
* TubeBuddy only allows testing one variation against the original at a time
* This eliminates the need for multiple rounds of testing and significantly speeds up the optimisation process

3. Advanced Analytics
[Thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) provides more sophisticated analytics using a Bayesian analysis approach
The Bayesian analysis approach is a statistical method that helps determine which thumbnail or title variation is most likely to be the best performer overall, rather than relying on a single metric like CTR.
Here's what makes Bayesian analysis special for thumbnail testing:
Probability-Based Decision Making: Rather than simply comparing raw numbers (like CTR), Bayesian analysis calculates the probability that each variation is truly the best option, considering all available data.
It looks at the relationship between:

* Click-through rate (CTR)
* Average view duration

[Thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) doesn't just look at CTR like TubeBuddy does. This is critical because:

* Avoids Clickbait Trap: A thumbnail might have high CTR because it's clickbaity, but if viewers immediately leave (low average view duration), YouTube's algorithm will eventually penalise the video.
* Aligns With YouTube's Algorithm: YouTube cares about engagement after the click, not just getting the click. Bayesian analysis helps find thumbnails that optimise for YouTube's actual ranking factors.

Why These Metrics Matter for Channel Growth:
The advanced analytics offered by[ thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) are critical for sustainable channel growth on YouTube. While TubeBuddy focuses primarily on CTR,[ thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/)'s "CTR*AVD Metric" considers both click-through rate and viewer retention metrics - average view duration.
This matters tremendously because YouTube's algorithm heavily prioritises videos that not only attract clicks but also maintain viewer engagement after those clicks. A high-CTR thumbnail that fails to deliver on its promise creates a poor viewer experience, leading to short watch times and significantly reduced recommendations by YouTube.
*By optimising for both metrics simultaneously, you're aligning your content strategy with YouTube's algorithmic preferences, resulting in more consistent recommendations and sustainable growth. The "CTR**AVD" metric helps you avoid the clickbait trap and instead identifies thumbnails & titles that create a virtuous cycle: better thumbnails lead to more clicks and higher AVD (average view duration), which lead to more recommendations, which lead to more views and channel growth.

Part 1: Using[ thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/)
1. Dashboard Overview
When you log in to[ thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/), you'll see four main tabs:

* Ongoing Tests: Currently running tests
* Past Tests: Completed or cancelled tests
* Draft: Saved test drafts
* Create New Test: Start a new test (also accessible via the button at the top)

2. Creating a New Test

* Click "Create New Test" button



* Select the video you want to test from your channel

If the video is a recent upload, you’ll have to manually click “Get New Videos” button at the top right
Or if you’re testing/changing titles, consider updating the videos database by clicking “Update All Videos Data”

* Choose the test type:

* Thumbnail test: Test different thumbnail designs
* Title test: Test different title variations
* Thumbnail and title test: Test both elements simultaneously

Let's start with “Thumbnail Only” testing first

3. Configuring Your Thumbnail Test
A. Uploading Variations

* You'll see the original thumbnail displayed first
* Click "Upload Variations" to add your alternative thumbnails



* You can upload as many variations as needed (no limit)
   * Each will be tested in rotation according to your test settings

B. Including the Original Thumbnail
After uploading variations and clicking “Next Step” , you'll see an option to include the original thumbnail in the test:

Include Original Thumbnail Option

* BEST PRACTICE (100% RECOMMENDED): Always include the original thumbnail
* Reasons for including the original:
   * It acts as a baseline for comparison
   * Without it, you won't know if your new variations actually perform better than what you already have
   * You might identify a winner among your variations, but it could still perform worse than the original

Use Past Data Option

* What it does: Uses the past 3-5 days of data for the original thumbnail/title instead of including it in the rotation
* How it works:
   * The system pulls data from YouTube Studio for the original thumbnail/title from the past few days
   * It only tests your new variations in rotation
   * At the end, it compares new variations to the historical data from the original
* Best Practice: We generally DO NOT recommend using this option
* Reasons to avoid:
   * External events or trends may impact performance during different testing periods
   * Example: If your video is about a recession and recession news breaks during testing of new variations, but not during the period of the original data, the test results would be skewed
   * Real-time comparison ensures all variations face the same external factors
   * Only consider using this if you need extremely quick results and are confident in your original baseline

4. Configuring Your Title Test

The process for title testing is similar to thumbnail testing:

* You'll see the original title displayed


* Add your title variations


* Choose whether to include the original (highly recommended)


* Arrange the testing sequence


5. Advanced Test Settings
A. Test Type Selection

* Time-based (Recommended): Test runs for a predetermined period
* Metric-based: Test runs until reaching a view threshold (not recommended as it's less optimised)


C. Test Format

* Classic (Default/Recommended): Runs different thumbnails each hour/day in a repeating sequence (A, B, C, D, A, B, C, D)
* Consecutive: Splits time evenly between thumbnails
   * Example: With 2 thumbnails tested over 24 hours, thumbnail A would show for 12 hours, then thumbnail B for 12 hours
   * The test length gets evenly distributed between variations
* Best Practice:
   * Use Classic for recent videos
   * Consider Consecutive for older videos

E. Test Speed and Duration
For New Uploads:

* Hourly Testing (Strongly Recommended)
   * For new uploads under 24hours - 48 hours old, always use hourly testing
   * Recommended setup: 4-hour tests (1 hour per variation) for immediate insights. Important: Focus on CTR and Average View Duration data rather than total views or impressions when analyzing results. The first thumbnail tested typically receives more views due to subscriber notifications, which can skew view-based metrics.
   * Example: With 4 variations + original, set 1 hour per variation
   * Critical Best Practice: Ensure testing occurs during peak audience hours for your channel. Avoid scheduling tests during off-hours (e.g., 1AM if your audience is primarily active during the day)
   * Why this matters: Different thumbnails should have an equal opportunity to be shown when your audience is most active.
   * After identifying a winner in the first few hours, you can end the test early and set that thumbnail/title. This approach optimises early performance when it matters most for the YouTube algorithm.
   * Pro Tip:[ thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) has a 1-hour buffer before tests begin. If you publish at 5:00 PM and schedule the test immediately after, it won't start until 7:00 PM. To work around this, publish your video at 4:55 PM and schedule the test within those 5 minutes - this way testing begins around 6:00 PM, approximately 1 hour after publishing.

For Older Videos (Rehashes):

* Daily Testing (Recommended)
   * This allows sufficient time for each variation to be tested thoroughly
   * The platform will suggest a test duration based on the number of variations
   * Example: 6 variations might suggest a 20-day test, 4 variations a 16-day test
   * You can adjust the duration as needed, but ensure each variation gets adequate testing time


F. Start Date and Time
This is for hourly only, for daily, tests start at midnight PST, as this is when YouTube analytics update

* The platform always gives you a one-hour buffer from the current time
* Example: If current time is 2 PM, the earliest available start time would be 4 PM
* Times are based on UTC, so be aware of time zone differences.


G. Auto-Set Winner

* Automatically sets the best-performing variation when the test concludes
* Currently limited to dropdown menu options - we recommend selecting CTR as the metric since the combined CTR×AVD metric is not available as an auto-set option
* This is convenient but consider reviewing the results yourself before implementation to ensure the winner aligns with the CTR×AVD performance rather than just the selected metric.


6. Understanding and Analysing Results
[thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) provides detailed analytics for each variation:
A. Key Performance Metrics
Click-Through Rate (CTR)

* Percentage of impressions that resulted in a click
* Important, but should not be the only factor in decision-making


Average View Duration

* How long viewers watched on average
* Critical for YouTube's algorithm
* Even small differences (10-15 seconds) can be significant for shorter videos


Watch Time

* Total hours the video was watched during the test period
* Can be higher for variations with higher CTR, even if average view duration is lower


Views

* Total number of views each variation received

Testing Periods

* When each variation was shown (date and time)
* Important for verifying fair testing conditions ****and cross tracking metrics on the YT studio to identify any peaks and knowing which variation caused that.

CTR × Average View Duration Score (CTR*AVD)

* A combined metric created by[ thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/)
* Calculated by multiplying CTR by average view duration
* Helps identify thumbnails that balance clickability and engagement
* Example: CTR (6.07%) × Average View Duration (369 seconds) × 10 = Score of 22,398


B. How to Interpret Results

* Don't just look at CTR - the highest CTR doesn't always mean best performance
* A thumbnail with high CTR but low retention might be clickbait and harm long-term performance
* The platform calculates a combined score (CTR × Average View Duration) to identify thumbnails that drive clicks AND retain viewers

C. Implementing Winners and Ending Tests
For new uploads:

* If a clear winner emerges in the first 4-5 hours with a significant performance difference, end the test early
* Click "Set Thumbnail" on the winning variation (this will mark the test as "cancelled")
* This allows YouTube to start optimising distribution with the best-performing thumbnail right away

For rehashes:

* Generally, allow the full test duration to ensure reliable results
* Implement the winner with the highest CTR*AVD
* Do not solely focus on the thumbnail with the highest CTR

Part 2: Strategic Approaches to Thumbnail and Title Testing
1. Thumbnail and Title Creation Process
A. For New Uploads
Pre-Upload Thumbnail Selection:

* Your team designs multiple thumbnail options (typically 5-6)
* In a team decision process, select the thumbnail you believe will perform best as the "original"
* This selection is based on team judgment and experience, not yet backed by data
* The other designs become your test variations

Post-Upload Testing:

* Include the original and 4-5 "backup" thumbnails as variations
* Start testing immediately upon upload with hourly tests
* Monitor the first 4-5 hours closely
* If a clear winner emerges, set it as the permanent thumbnail

Best Practice: For new uploads, focus on quick thumbnail optimization within the first 24-48 hours to maximize early video performance and avoid confusing YouTube's algorithm with extended testing of poorly performing variations.
B. For Established Videos (Rehashes)
When to Rehash Videos:

* Underperforming Videos: Videos that should be performing better based on content quality and topic
* Already Successful Videos: To further boost already well-performing content
   * Example: A video consistently in your top 10 performing videos could potentially perform even better with optimised thumbnails/titles
   * Even a small improvement in a high-performing video can yield significant view increases
* Outdated Visual Elements: When thumbnails contain outdated elements:
   * Outdated trends or references (e.g., a thumbnail referencing ChatGPT when newer AI tools exist)
   * Outdated tools or interfaces are shown (e.g., old social media interfaces)
   * Outdated appearance of the creator (e.g., significant changes in appearance like hair, beard)
   * Outdated year references (e.g., "2023" in the thumbnail when it's now 2025)

Important Note on Creator Appearance: When updating thumbnails for older videos:

* Try to use images of the creator that match their appearance in that video
* Avoid using current images for very old videos, as this could create a disconnect when viewers click
* Instead, update other elements like text, graphics, or background while maintaining temporal consistency

2. Advanced Testing Strategy
A. Sequential Testing Approach
If you are confident on the original title, and it matches your video’s content (not clickbait) then:

* Test thumbnails first
   * This is always the starting point as thumbnails typically have a significant impact on CTR
   * Since you already have multiple thumbnail variations, start with these
* If thumbnail changes don't improve performance:
   * If the original thumbnail keeps winning or improvements are minimal
   * AND the video is still underperforming, move to title testing
   * BUT if the video is performing well despite no thumbnail improvement, you can keep the original
* If both thumbnail and title testing don't work:
   * Test title + thumbnail combinations to try completely different approaches
   * This allows you to experiment with entirely new concepts and messaging
* Process of elimination:
   * If you've tested various thumbnails and the original continues to win, but the video is underperforming
   * This indicates the thumbnail is likely not the issue, and you should focus on the title instead

B. Title Testing Considerations

* Recent trends show a shift from formal titles to more authentic, conversational titles
* Consider testing both approaches:
   * Traditional format: "How to Make $10,000 With This Method in 2025"
   * Newer style: "Give me XX mins, I’ll show you how to make $XXX/day with [TOPIC] (2025)”
* For title rehashing, we often focus on well-performing videos to boost them further

C. Thumbnail Design Tips

* Ensure thumbnail matches the actual video content (avoid clickbait that hurts retention)
* For creator-focused channels, use current images of the creator for new videos
* When rehashing older videos, use images that match their appearance from that time period
* Consider current trends in thumbnail design but avoid overused formats

3. Specific Testing Scenarios
A. New Upload Strategy (First 24 Hours - 48 Hours)
Step 1: Initial Setup

* Upload video with your team's selected "best" thumbnail
* Immediately set up hourly testing with 4-5 variations
* Set to 1 hour per variation (4-5 hours total cycle)

Step 2: Early Monitoring

* Monitor performance during the first complete cycle
* Look for significant differences in CTR and the Views per minute/hour on YT studio

Step 3: Quick Implementation

* If a clear winner emerges with significantly better metrics, end the test
* Implement the winner immediately
* This gives YouTube a consistent thumbnail to promote during the critical early period

Step 4: Continue Optimisation (Optional)

* After 48 hours, if performance isn't satisfactory, consider a follow-up test
* This could be additional thumbnail variations or title testing
* For these follow-up tests, you can use 2-hour or 4-hour rotations

B. Rehash Strategy for Older Videos
Step 1: Video Selection

* Identify videos for rehashing based on performance data and potential
* Prioritise both underperforming videos with good content and already successful videos that could perform better

Step 2: Thumbnail Testing

* Design 3-5 new thumbnail variations
* Set up daily testing for 2 weeks
* Include the original thumbnail

Step 3: Results Analysis

* After the test completes, analyse the full suite of metrics
* Focus on the CTR*AVD metric
* Implement the winner if it outperforms the original significantly

Step 4: Title Testing (If Needed)

* If thumbnail testing doesn't yield significant improvements:
   * Create 4-6 title variations
   * Test for 2 weeks
   * Analyse using the same metrics approach

4. Monitoring and Optimisation Cycle

* Regular Performance Review:
   * Review your channel's video performance weekly
   * Look for videos with declining performance or untapped potential
* Identify Candidates for Testing:
   * Recently uploaded videos with lower-than-expected performance
   * Older videos with potential for renewed interest
   * High-performing videos that could perform even better
   * Videos with outdated visual elements or references
* Test, Analyse, Implement:
   * Follow the testing process outlined above
   * Always base decisions on the CTR*AVD metric
   * Document your changes and expected outcomes
* Review Results:
   * After implementing changes, monitor performance for 1-2 weeks
   * Compare pre- and post-optimisation metrics
* Repeat As Needed:
   * Continue the optimisation cycle
   * Some videos may benefit from multiple rounds of optimisation over time

Conclusion: Best Practices Summary

* Always include the original in your tests for proper baseline comparison (100% recommended)
* Trust the CTR*AVD metrics over simple CTR
   * This balances click-attractiveness with viewer retention
   * Avoids the clickbait trap that can harm channel performance
* Use appropriate testing speeds
   * Hourly testing for new uploads (first 48 hours)
   * Daily testing for rehashes of older content
* Consider both CTR and retention metrics when evaluating results
   * Remember that YouTube's algorithm rewards videos that keep viewers watching
   * The CTR × Average View Duration score is a valuable indicator of overall performance
* Test during peak hours for your audience to ensure fair comparison
   * All variations should have an equal opportunity to be seen by your active audience
   * Avoid testing critical variations during off-hours
* Be willing to end tests early for new uploads if a clear winner emerges
   * This optimises performance during the critical early period
   * Manual implementation can be done by clicking "Set Thumbnail"
* Maintain a regular rehashing schedule for continuous channel optimisation
   * Both underperforming and successful videos can benefit from rehashing
   * Consider temporal relevance and current trends when updating older videos
* Ensure visual consistency between thumbnails and video content
   * Use images of creators that match their appearance in the video
   * Update outdated elements while maintaining temporal consistency

By following this comprehensive guide, you'll be able to leverage[ thumbnailtest.com](http://thumbnailtest.com/) to its fullest potential, achieving higher CTR, better retention, and ultimately more views and growth for your YouTube channel.
